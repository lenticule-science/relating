# Typing, TypeBox, the IR, and our typed-total CHL LISP

This note is the conceptual map of the architecture `lib/relating` (and now `lib/knowing`) settled into.
It assumes nothing and connects everything: why we author data with TypeBox, why a faculty's *logic* is an intermediate representation rather than hand-written code, why that IR is a typed-total LISP that *already existed proven in Lean*, how a type-checker turns it into a proof calculus, and how all of it collapsed a pile of seemingly-separate problems into one structure.
The companion notes carry the mechanical status ([master plan](2026-06-22-faculty-refactor-master-plan.md)), the deeper *why* of the term layer ([handoff](2026-06-22-ir-term-layer-and-polyglot-handoff.md)), and the idiom ([faculty idiom](2026-06-22-the-faculty-package-idiom-conceptual-target.md)); this one is the synthesis a cold reader should start from.

## 0. The one problem: drift between three copies of the same thing

A faculty (relating, knowing, …) has to say what its *data* is and what its *operations* do, and it has to say it in more than one place: a runtime validator, a static TypeScript type, a JSON Schema for the OpenAPI/MCP faces — and, because we *prove* our mathematics, again in Lean.
Every hand-maintained copy is a drift surface.
The whole architecture is the answer to one question: **how do you say each thing exactly once and have everything else be a projection?**

Two halves:

- **Data is single-sourced by TypeBox** — one authored value is the type *and* the schema *and* the validator.
- **Logic is single-sourced by an IR** — one authored term is the operation, projected to every runtime, and checked against Lean.

## 1. TypeBox: one value is the type, the schema, and the validator

A carrier is authored once:

```ts
const utteranceCarrier = carrier("relating/articulating/utterance", { … });
```

That single value yields, with no code generation:

- the **static TypeScript type** (`Static<typeof carrier.schema>`),
- the **JSON Schema** document, identified by a bare path-form `$id`,
- the **Ajv runtime validator**.

The `*.json` schema files the OpenAPI/MCP transports serve are *outputs* serialized from the registry, never hand-authored inputs.
This replaced an earlier codegen scheme (`*.schema.json` + a generated `*.schema.types.mts` + a hand-written validator), which had three artifacts to keep in sync, a bootstrap cycle, and gitignored outputs a clean clone couldn't rebuild.
The lesson that generalizes: **drift is eliminated by collapsing artifacts, not by tooling that re-syncs them.**

A morphism is authored the same way: `action({ domain, codomain, run | runTerm })` reads the `$id`s off the carrier objects, so **composition is a schema identity equation** — `composes(p, c)` is exactly `p.codomain.id === c.domain.id`.
Discovery, the four transports (CLI/HTTP/OpenAPI/MCP), and carrier-node mounting all fall out of these declarations.

### Carriers present their generators (free ⊣ forgetful)

A carrier past the early stages is a **free construction** over a small generating set (a predication site is the free category over its cells+specializations; a settlement is generated by closing charges).
A free object carries nothing beyond its generators, so the stored/wire form keeps only them and the derived structure is rebuilt on read: `present` forgets to the generators, `free` rebuilds the full object, `free ∘ present ≅ id`.
This *is* the corpus law "**no free data past Mark and Articulating**" read from the engineering side — the carriers are *freely constructed*, which is *why* they carry no *free data* (same word, two senses, one fact).
Concretely it turned 3 KB derived-carrier monsters into tidy structural terms (the predication site went 2947 → 448 bytes), which is what made the next half — expressing morphisms as IR terms — tractable at all.

## 2. The IR: a faculty's logic is data, not code

A morphism's *data* was single-sourced, but its *logic* was still written twice — proven in Lean (`RelMath`) and executed in TypeScript.
The fix is to write each morphism once, in a small **intermediate representation**, and project it to the runtimes.
A `runTerm` is that: the four faces all run the *same* IR through one `dispatch`, so a morphism's logic is language-neutral data, not per-transport code.

The right IR is a **typed, total LISP, authored as data (JSON s-expressions)**:

- **LISP**, because predication is nested tagged structure (s-expressions), wants a tiny derivable core, and — the deep reason — **`eval` IS settling**.
  The corpus says computation = predication reaching a fixed point; LISP evaluation is reduction to normal form.
  The evaluator is the engine looking at itself; homoiconicity (code is data) is the metacircular reflex made free — an IR term is a carrier, so the engine can hold, witness, and serve its own operations.
- **Typed and total**, because the kernel is schema-first and Lean is typed, and because totality (recursion only through proven folds) is what makes every term provable and safely projectable.
- **Authored as data**, so version one needs no parser: you build the AST-as-carrier with combinators, exactly as TypeBox authors schemas as values.

## 3. The payoff that made it real: the IR already existed, proven, in Lean

The IR is not invented.
`RelMath.
CHL.
Tm` (`sites/relmath.bare-cedar-fog.emsenn.net/RelMath/CurryHowardLambekQuartet.lean`) is a complete de-Bruijn **simply-typed lambda calculus** with products and unit, with substitution lemmas and **Church–Rosser confluence proven** (Takahashi method).
Its types `base/arrow/prod/unit` are proven ≅ the recurring FOUR. de Bruijn indices were chosen *by discovery* — the corpus already proved its substitution calculus on them.
So the TS (and eventually Rust) evaluators are faithful shadows of a Lean-proven calculus; `evaluate`/`dev`/`step` are golden-vector-checked against Lean's `dev`/`step`.

The primitive set is two layers, both in Lean:

- **Core (special forms), proven:** types `base/arrow/prod/unit`; terms `var/lam/app/pair/ fst/snd/star`; β/π reduction, confluent.
- **Built-ins (primitive procedures), the domain at the no-free-data floor:** `mark` (the irreducible atom), the FOUR `charge` algebra (`negate`/`conflate`/`joinK`/`meetK`/`leK`/…), and `settle` (the closure to a fixed point).

To express the faculties' actual operations we added a handful of **structural primitives**, each justified by a specific morphism it unlocked: `field` (named projection over the bridge's object encoding), `append` (list concat), `coincide` (structural equality → bool), `cond` (conditional), and `fold` — the **one structural recursion** (a left fold of a curried combiner over an array term, reducing by `fold f a (cons h t) = fold f (f a h) t`, the apps reducing by the existing β-rule; no TS delegation).
Over `fold` the von Neumann naturals are *derived*, not primitive: `count = fold succ 0`, byte-identical to the carrier's exact-number `count`.

## 4. The type-checker makes the IR a proof calculus (Curry–Howard)

`hasTy(ctx, term, type)` is `RelMath.
CHL.
HasTy` made decidable — a bidirectional checker: introduction forms (`lam`/`pair`/`star`) are **checked** against a type, elimination forms (`var`/`app`/`fst`/`snd`) **infer** theirs (with a small extra rule so bare-`lam` redexes type, making it complete, not just for normal forms).
Typing covers the CHL core only — the domain primitives are not propositions.

This is the hinge that turns the IR from "a calculus we evaluate" into "a calculus we *prove* in": by Curry–Howard, **a well-typed CHL-core term IS a proof of its type-as-proposition.** `hasTy` is the **trusted proof-verification floor** — the small LCF-style kernel a proof *search* is checked against.
A found proof is trustworthy because it type-checks, not because the search that produced it is.

## 5. Lean grounding is three layers, not one

Conflating these is what made early attempts ship hollow vectors.
The IR's authority is Lean, but *which* Lean, and *how*, differs by layer:

1. **Generic calculus** (the CHL core, the FOUR algebra, `hasTy`, the regime/cohomology invariants `classify`/`cohomologyVanishes`) — **golden-vectored against Lean**: `conformance/*.lean` emits EAVT rows from the real RelMath defs, committed as fixtures, replayed through the TS evaluator/terms.
  This is the part Lean *has*.
2. **Descent / carrier-serialization terms** (`articulate…settle`, `free`, the readers' carrier shapes) — these rebuild *relating's own carrier serialization*, which RelMath does **not** have (RelMath has the abstract math: Mark, Articulating, Charge).
  So they are *correctly* checked **differential against the real TS op on the real carrier** (`deepEqual` + canonical wire), never against a toy Lean model.
  Trying to Lean-vector them was the hollow attempt's error.
3. **Faithfulness is on the wire.** Carriers are JSON over transports, so the gate is *canonical bytes* (and the parsed wire form), not `deepStrictEqual` — the real op may carry class-instance values where the IR rebuilds plain objects; identical serialized, distinct prototypes.

The discipline this enforces: **faithfulness is checked against the real op on the real carrier, never self-consistency.** A passing test that compares the IR to a wrapper of itself proves nothing; the differential check must bite when the term is wrong.

## 6. The arrow/boundary gate

"Which runs must be IR?" is decided by the corpus's own arrow/boundary distinction, by a *computable* check, not a curated list: **a run must be an IR term iff the action is a true morphism — both domain and codomain are admitted carriers.** *Boundary* actions are exempt by nature: **admission** (raw input → carrier: `utter`, parsing a proposition) and **transport/codecs** (carrier → wire/text: `realize`, the content reader).
The gate flips shut on its own as the last morphisms land; a test enforces it.

The genuinely hard tier this gated — the nine `read-*-of-settlement-filtration` measurement arrows — is instructive: unlike the structural descent, they *compute* an invariant (the codomain *is* the number), so they needed `fold` **and the settling closure materialized inside the IR**.
Over the descent's flat, spec-free domain that closure is provably a single initial stage (the seed is already closed), so the readers' interesting branches are unreachable and the readers are faithful over exactly the domain the actions accept — documented, not faked.

## 7. knowing: the CHL correspondence as a faculty

`lib/knowing` is the first downstream faculty rebuilt on all of this, and it shows the architecture is not just plumbing — it *is* the mathematics of the domain. **To know is the Curry–Howard–Lambek correspondence, read two ways**, and they are the *same* structure (Wurm: concept lattices are complete semantics for the Lambek calculus):

- **Syntax — holding a proof.** `find` is **proof search** over the CHL.
  Tm calculus, every witness **certified by `hasTy`**.
  The proofs it searches *are* the IR terms.
  Knowing a proposition = inhabiting its type.
- **Semantics — forming a concept.** The corpus is a **formal context** (documents = objects, frontmatter = attributes); `conceptualize` forms **concepts** = closed `(extent, intent)` fixed points of the **Galois closure**, which *is* the **settling closure** `Cl = □◇` (`FCALambekCircle`).
  Knowing a region = computing its concept lattice = running the closure.

Both rest on the settling closure as a **coalgebra** (`c : E → F_U(E)` — close under inference, then admit new evidence): a known fact is a *fixed point* of the closure (property, not data — "no free data" read epistemically), and the modal pair **◇ ⊣ □** is its two halves.

knowing also taught the **capability-surface** discipline: most of what its old `corpus/` module did — walking the filesystem, decoding markdown, projecting to the wire — was a *poor re-enactment* of capabilities relating already ships (its codecs, its holography).
The fix was to **grow relating to own the missing half** (the content-tier *reader*, the inverse of the writer it already had) and **shrink knowing to consume it** — so knowing's corpus reading imports no `node:fs`; the navigation *logic* is knowing's, the I/O is relating's.
The rule: when you find a faculty re-enacting infrastructure, complete the capability upstream and consume it; never keep the re-enactment.

## 8. What it all unified

The reason this architecture is worth the trouble is that one structure turned out to be many things at once — the Curry–Howard–Lambek quartet, recurring:

| reading | the same thing |
| --- | --- |
| **proofs** | well-typed CHL.Tm terms (`hasTy`) |
| **programs** | IR `runTerm`s a faculty's morphisms reduce to |
| **morphisms** | the actions, composing by carrier `$id` |
| **propositions** | the types / the carriers |
| **concepts** | closed fixed points of the closure (FCA) |
| **knowledge** | fixed points of the settling closure (`Cl v = v`) |

and one operation, recurring:

> **`eval` = `reduce` = `settle` = `close`.**

Reducing an IR term to normal form, settling a valuation to its fixed point, and closing a formal context to its concepts are the same act — which is why the IR's evaluator, the settling engine, and FCA concept formation are one piece of mathematics with three faces, and why "computation = settling" is the same joke as "eval = reduce."

## 9. The polyglot horizon (why single-source, ultimately)

Single-sourcing *data* (schemas) and *logic* (IR) makes the workspace **language-neutral at the contract layer**.
The TypeBox-emitted JSON Schema is an IDL (TS via `Static<>`, Rust via `typify`, Lean via `Lean.
Json`); an s-expr evaluator is trivial in any of the three, and golden vectors over IR terms catch divergence.
So a future Rust/Wasm port is "re-implement the `eval`/data-plane behind the same frozen contract," not a rewrite — and Lean stays the *proof authority*, the trusted floor of a tower (not a loop: trust flows one way down to the small LCF kernel).
"Single source of *code*" across proof and runtime isn't achievable today (no push-button Lean extraction); "single source of *truth*" — the schema for data, the proven laws for logic — is, and nothing drifts without a red test.

## The one-paragraph version

Author data once as a TypeBox carrier (type + schema + validator); author logic once as an IR term (a typed-total LISP that is a JSON shadow of the Lean-proven `CHL.
Tm`); store only generators because everything past Mark and Articulating is a free construction; make a run an IR term iff it is a true morphism (admission/transport are boundaries); add `hasTy` and the IR becomes a proof calculus where terms are proofs; ground the generic calculus and the measurement math against Lean and the carrier-serialization differentially against the real TS op; and watch it collapse proofs, programs, morphisms, propositions, concepts, and knowledge into one structure whose single operation — `eval = reduce = settle = close` — is the engine looking at itself.
